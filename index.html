01 / THE MEDIA MAP

Six channels.
One connected world.

Media is the channel or format used to communicate information. Each type has strengths, limits, and a different effect on its audience. Select a card to explore.

TYPE / 01
▤

Print Media

Information presented through printed words and images.

NEWSPAPERSBOOKSPOSTERS
Strength: Can be kept, annotated, and read without a device.
Limit: Updates may be slow, and printed material can become outdated.
Our take: A printed page can feel trustworthy, but the date and author still matter.

◖)))

Broadcast Media

Audio and video distributed to a wide audience at the same time.

RADIOTVPUBLIC SERVICE
Strength: Reaches many people quickly and can communicate through sound and visuals.
Limit: A short segment may leave out context.
Our take: A confident voice is not automatically a correct one.

▣

Digital Media

Content created, stored, and accessed using electronic devices.

WEBSITESE-BOOKSEMAIL
Strength: Searchable, shareable, and often updated quickly.
Limit: Anyone can publish; quality varies.
Our take: Convenience should never replace checking the source.
TYPE / 04
◎

New / Social Media

Interactive platforms where audiences can also create and respond to content.

SOCIAL APPSBLOGSLIVESTREAMS
Strength: Gives people a voice and helps communities connect.
Limit: Trends and algorithms can reward speed over accuracy.
Our take: A post with thousands of reactions still needs evidence.
TYPE / 05
▶

Film & Video Media

Stories and information communicated through moving images and sound.

FILMSDOCUMENTARIESVIDEO ESSAYS
Strength: Combines images, sound, and storytelling to make ideas memorable.
Limit: Editing, music, and camera choices can influence emotion.
Our take: Ask what the video shows—and what it leaves outside the frame.

▱

Outdoor Media

Messages displayed in public spaces and shared physical environments.

BILLBOARDSBANNERSPOSTERS
Strength: Visible to people in everyday places without needing internet access.
Limit: Has little room for detail and may simplify a complex issue.
Our take: Advertising is designed to persuade, so notice its purpose.

04 / DIGITAL CODE

Good signals.
Better citizens.

Being media-literate is not only about spotting unreliable information. It is also about how we treat people and participate online.

01

Remember the person.
There is a real human being behind every account and comment.

02

Think before posting.
Pause before publishing something that could harm or embarrass someone.

03

Respect privacy.
Ask permission before sharing someone's personal details, photos, or messages.

04

Credit creators.
Acknowledge original work instead of presenting it as your own.

05

Disagree with respect.
Challenge ideas without insulting the people who express them.

06

Correct mistakes.
If you share something inaccurate, update or correct it clearly.

07

Protect your account.
Use strong, unique passwords and keep private information secure.

08

Don't feed harmful trends.
Avoid amplifying harassment, rumors, or content that targets others.

GROUP REFLECTION / FIRST-PERSON VIEW

“Being informed is not the same as knowing the truth.”

As students, we use media for almost everything: schoolwork, entertainment, communication, and keeping up with what is happening around us. We realized that having access to information does not automatically mean we understand it. Sometimes a headline catches our attention, a video changes our mood, or a post gets shared so often that we start accepting it without checking.

Our biggest insight is that media is powerful because it can shape what we notice, what we believe, and how we respond to other people. We should not treat every platform as completely good or completely bad. Instead, we need to understand its purpose, recognize its limits, and decide how to use it responsibly.

As a group, we believe that being a responsible media user means slowing down. We should compare sources, check dates, look for evidence, respect other people's privacy, and admit when we are wrong. We are not expected to know everything—but we are responsible for how we handle what we know.

Our takeaway: Don't let the speed of information decide the quality of your thinking. Be curious, be critical, and be responsible.
